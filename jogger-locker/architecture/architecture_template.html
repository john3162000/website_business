UP Oval Locker System

How a jogger rents and opens a locker with no staff on site. A small computer inside the kiosk runs the doors and checks PINs, even with no internet. A cloud app takes QR Ph payments through PayMongo and texts PINs through Semaphore. You watch and fix everything from your phone.

  • Pay by dynamic QR Ph, never by receipt.PayMongo tells the server when the money arrives, so nobody checks screenshots and a fake receipt opens nothing.
  • The kiosk opens lockers on its own.It keeps the active rentals and PIN hashes locally, so people get their things back when the internet or server is down. Only renting needs the internet.
  • Never hold belongings hostage.If the kiosk can't take an overtime payment, it opens the door anyway and records what is owed.
  • One HTTPS connection, no message broker.The kiosk calls the server and keeps one request open to hear commands within a second. That is one less service to run and pay for. Add MQTT only past about 10 kiosks.
  • Raspberry Pi, not an Android tablet.It drives the lock boards and the database directly, keeps time through outages, and has no battery to swell in the heat.
  • Off-the-shelf lock boards.RS485 boards with door sensors are what commercial smart lockers use. An ESP32 with relays is fine for a bench test but harder to keep running for years.
  • Reuse the stack you already have.Next.js, Prisma and Supabase Postgres, like platform/ in this repo, hosted on Render in Singapore.
  • Every remote action leaves a trail.Remote opens, refunds and disabled doors need a reason and go into a log you can check later.

When something breaks

With no staff on site, every failure needs a planned answer. Wherever a row says alert, you get an SMS.

What failsWhat the jogger seesWhat the system does
Internet or server down"Renting is paused. Opening your locker still works."Checks PINs on the kiosk and queues events. The server sends you an alert after 3 minutes without a heartbeat; an outside uptime monitor watches the server itself.
Power cutNothing, for several hoursThe UPS runs everything; alert sent. Below 20% battery, renting pauses. If the battery runs out, doors stay locked until someone uses the override key.
Paid, but the door won't open"Door 12 failed. We moved you to door 14."Tries another free door of the same size. If none is free, refunds through PayMongo, texts the jogger and marks the door faulty; alert sent.
Door left openA beep and "Please close door 12"Alert after 5 minutes. The rental stays active.
Door forced open—Alarm and alert; check the CCTV.
Forgot the PIN"Resend PIN" buttonTexts the PIN again to the same number, up to 3 times.
Dead or lost phoneHotline number on the kioskYou check the details and the CCTV, then open the door from the dashboard with a logged reason.
Someone guessing PINsDoor locked for 15 minutes after 5 wrong triesAlert sent.
Things left over 24 hoursSMS remindersStaff empty the locker, photograph the items and keep them for 30 days.
Kiosk computer freezesBlank screen for about a minuteA hardware watchdog restarts it. If it is dead, open doors with the override key and swap in the spare memory card.
Payment confirmation arrives lateThe QR screen keeps waitingThe kiosk also asks the server directly, and a nightly check matches every PayMongo payment to a rental.

Security and privacy

  • Payments: only PayMongo's signed webhook, or the server asking PayMongo directly, can mark a rental paid. Each webhook is handled once, even if PayMongo sends it twice.
  • PINs: 6 random digits. The server sends a PIN once to the kiosk and once by SMS, and both keep only a salted hash. Five wrong tries lock that door for 15 minutes and alert you.
  • Commands: the kiosk has its own secret. Every command is signed and expires after 60 seconds, so a copied or replayed command cannot open a door.
  • Your dashboard: a strong password and two-factor login. Every remote open asks for a reason and is logged.
  • The kiosk: kiosk mode, a read-only system disk and no open ports. You reach it through Tailscale. The service cabinet has a tamper switch.
  • Secrets: database, PayMongo and Semaphore keys live in Render's environment settings, never in the repo.
  • Data Privacy Act of 2012: show a short privacy notice at the kiosk, ask consent for the mobile number, delete numbers after 90 days, and never show a full number on screen.

Build plan

Durations are rough guesses for one person working part time.

  1. Bench prototype, 2–3 weeks. A Pi, the screen, one lock board and 4 locks on a table, with a fake "paid" button. Done when renting, opening by PIN and opening with the network unplugged all work.
  2. Payments and SMS, 2–3 weeks. The server on Render, the database, PayMongo in test mode, the webhook and Semaphore. Done when a real ₱20 QR payment opens a door and the PIN arrives by SMS within 10 seconds.
  3. Owner tools, 1–2 weeks. Door grid, rentals, refunds, remote open with a reason, alerts and a daily summary. Done when you can run a whole day from your phone.
  4. Install and test on site, 2–4 weeks. Wire all 30 doors, the UPS and the 4G router. Test for a week by cycling doors, cutting power and unplugging the network. Then open with someone on site for the first two weekends. Done when a week passes with no manual fixes.
