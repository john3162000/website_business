# Grilling and roasting (inihaw, lechon)

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · compare [[Baking]], [[Deep frying]]

**Grilling** is cooking over direct heat (charcoal *ihaw*). **Roasting** is cooking in hot dry air (oven, or whole-pig lechon over coals).

## What happens
- **Water and fat drip out.** Meat loses ~20–40% of its weight (yield ~0.6–0.8). Fat losses vary by cut; USDA (2014) lists fat change per cut and method.
- **Drippings carry some B vitamins and minerals** away unless they are made into sauce or basting.
- **Heme iron:** cooking reduces it by 1–43% depending on meat and method (Lombardi-Boccia et al. 2002).
- **Maillard browning** on the surface: flavour, some lysine loss, especially with sugary marinades (BBQ, inasal annatto oil with sugar). See [[Protein]].
- **Charring** forms heterocyclic amines (HCAs) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), compounds linked to cancer risk in large amounts. *A safety issue, not a nutrient-retention issue.*
- **Vegetables** (inihaw na talong, mais): dry heat, no leaching. Vitamin C ~60–90%; carotenoids kept.

## Typical retention (meat, drippings not eaten)
Thiamin ~**60–85%**, riboflavin and niacin ~**80–95%**, B12 ~**70–90%**, minerals ~**85–100%**, protein ~100%, fat **0.7–1.0** of raw (fatty cuts lose more).
