# Sautéing and stir-frying (ginisa)

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · compare [[Pan frying]], [[Steaming]]

**Sautéing** (Filipino *gisa*) is cooking in a little oil over high heat, usually starting with garlic, onion and tomato. **Stir-frying** is the same with constant tossing. Many Filipino dishes start with *ginisa* and then add water (ginisang monggo, ginisang upo, pancit), so they become [[Simmering and Stewing]].

## What happens
- **Short time, little water**: less leaching than boiling.
- **High pan temperature and cut surfaces**: some vitamin C oxidation. Yuan et al. 2009 found stir-frying and stir-fry-then-boil gave significant vitamin C and glucosinolate losses in broccoli (stir-fry + boil was the worst).
- **Oil makes carotenoids available**: stir-frying raised measured beta-carotene 2–3× in most leafy greens tested, including kangkong (Malaysian study; >100% values reflect easier extraction). Fat in the meal also raises absorption (Brown et al. 2004). See [[Vitamin A and Carotenoids]].
- Minerals and protein: nearly fully kept (Bognár 1998 for frying in general).

## Typical retention
Vitamin C ~**60–85%** (short cooking, whole leaves) to ~**50%** (long, small pieces); folate ~70–90%; minerals ~95–100%; carotenoids ~90–100% content, absorption up.

## Filipino dishes
Adobong kangkong, ginisang ampalaya, ginisang pechay, chopsuey, pancit canton/bihon toppings, ginisang sayote, ginisang togue.
