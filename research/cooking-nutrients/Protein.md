# Protein

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]]

## Short answer

Cooking keeps nearly all the protein (**retention ≈ 95–100%**). It usually makes protein **easier to digest**. Very hard or long dry heat can make a small part less useful.

## What happens

- **Denaturation:** heat unfolds protein chains. This is not a loss. Unfolded proteins are easier for digestive enzymes to reach.
  - **Cooked egg protein was ~91% digested vs ~51% for raw egg** (Evenepoel et al. 1998, 5 ileostomy patients; a later study with 10 healthy people found ~94% vs ~65%). *Moderate evidence; small studies, but consistent.*
  - Meat heated to ~70 °C was digested faster in the lab; above 100 °C, oxidation made proteins clump (aggregate) and slowed early digestion, though total digestibility was not worse (Bax et al. 2012, in vitro).
- **Maillard reaction:** at high dry heat (grilling, roasting, deep-frying crust, toasting), the amino acid **lysine** reacts with sugars and some becomes unavailable. Standard lab analysis still counts it as "lysine", so tables can overstate useful lysine in browned foods (Hurrell & Carpenter 1981). Losses are larger with sugar present (tocino, longganisa, glazed lechon skin, sweet BBQ marinades). Usually a small share of the total protein.
- **Leaching:** a little soluble protein moves into broth and drippings. Eaten broth keeps it.
- **Frying** has little or no effect on protein content (Fillion & Henry 1998; Bognár 1998).
- **Antinutrients in legumes** (trypsin inhibitors, lectins) are destroyed by proper boiling, which **improves** protein value. Undercooked kidney beans are toxic. See [[Antinutrients]].

## Filipino angle
- **Kinilaw** "cooks" fish with vinegar: the acid denatures surface proteins (the fish turns opaque) without heat. See [[Acid cooking (kinilaw)]].
- **Overcooked** meat (very long adobo, dried-out inihaw) gets tough and loses water, but protein grams are kept.
