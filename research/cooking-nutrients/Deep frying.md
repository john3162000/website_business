# Deep frying

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see [[Fat and Frying]], compare [[Pan frying]]

**Deep frying** means fully immersing food in oil at about 160–190 °C.

## What happens
- **Fat goes up** from absorbed oil (Bognár 1998: 2–14 g per 100 g raw food, higher with batter or breading). Fatty, un-breaded meat can **lose** fat. See [[Fat and Frying]].
- **Water leaves** the surface fast (yield factor below 1), so per-100 g values rise even though totals don't.
- **Vitamins:** the inside of the food stays near 100 °C and the cooking time is short, so frying often keeps **more** B1, B2, B6 and C than boiling, steaming or stewing (Bognár 1998). French fries keep vitamin C close to raw potato (Fillion & Henry 1998).
- **Protein and minerals:** little or no change (Fillion & Henry 1998).
- **Crust:** Maillard browning lowers available lysine at the surface. See [[Protein]].
- **Reused oil:** oxidation, vitamin E loss. See [[Vitamins D, E and K]].

## Typical retention
Vitamin C ~**60–90%**, thiamin ~**70–90%**, minerals ~**95–100%**, protein ~100%, vitamin A ~**75–95%**.

## Filipino dishes
Pritong isda, fried chicken, lumpiang shanghai, okoy, turon, banana cue (fried in sugar), chicharon, lechon kawali, bagnet, calamares, kropek, tokwa't baboy (fried tofu).
