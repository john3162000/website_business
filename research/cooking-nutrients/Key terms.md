# Key terms

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]].

**Raw (as purchased) vs edible portion (EP).** The edible portion is the part you eat, after removing peel, bones, seeds and so on. FNRI values are "per 100 g edible portion". SarapSulit already handles this with `epPct`.

**Yield factor (YF).** Cooked weight ÷ raw weight. Rice takes up water (YF ≈ 2.5–3; SarapSulit uses 2.76 from FNRI rows A019/A020). Meat loses water and fat (YF ≈ 0.6–0.8). A yield factor changes the *concentration* (per 100 g) but not the *total amount* of a nutrient. Sources: USDA Agriculture Handbook 102, USDA Cooking Yields for Meat and Poultry (2014), Bognár (2002). See [[Retention factor datasets]].

**Retention factor (RF).** The share of a nutrient still in the food after cooking, from 0 to 1 (or 0–100%). An RF of 0.5 for vitamin C means half is left.

**True retention (TR).** The correct way to measure an RF. It compares *totals*, so water gain or loss does not distort it (Murphy, Criner & Gray 1975):

> TR (%) = (nutrient per g cooked × g cooked) ÷ (nutrient per g raw × g raw) × 100

**Apparent retention.** Comparing nutrient per 100 g before and after cooking, without correcting for weight change. It is misleading: boiled spinach loses water-soluble vitamins but also gains water, so per-100 g figures mix both effects. The USDA table uses true retention. See [[Recipe calculation methods]].

**Leaching.** Nutrients dissolving out of the food into the cooking water. This is the main way minerals, vitamin C, B vitamins and some antinutrients leave the food. If the water (the *sabaw*) is eaten, most of what leached is still eaten. See [[Boiling]].

**Degradation (destruction).** The nutrient molecule itself breaks down because of heat, oxygen (oxidation), light, or high/low pH. Minerals can't be destroyed. Vitamins C, B1 and folate are destroyed easily. See [[Vitamin C]], [[Thiamin (B1)]].

**Bioavailability.** The share of an eaten nutrient the body actually absorbs and uses. **Bioaccessibility** is the share released from the food in the gut so it *can* be absorbed. Cooking can lower content but raise bioavailability (carotenoids, protein, minerals after phytate loss). See [[Vitamin A and Carotenoids]], [[Antinutrients]].

**Antinutrients.** Natural compounds that reduce absorption: phytate (phytic acid), oxalate, lectins, polyphenols/tannins. See [[Antinutrients]].

**Maillard reaction.** Browning between proteins (mostly the amino acid lysine) and sugars at high, dry heat. It gives flavour and colour; it makes some lysine unavailable. See [[Protein]].

**Gelatinization and retrogradation.** Starch granules swell and soften in hot water (gelatinization). On cooling, some starch re-crystallizes (retrogradation) and becomes "resistant starch" that acts like fiber. See [[Carbohydrates, Starch and Fiber]].

**Retinol equivalents (RE) / retinol activity equivalents (RAE).** Units that combine preformed vitamin A (retinol, from animal foods) and provitamin A carotenoids (mainly beta-carotene, from plants). FNRI reports vitamin A as retinol equivalents. See [[Vitamin A and Carotenoids]].
