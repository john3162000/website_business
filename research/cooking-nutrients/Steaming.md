# Steaming

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · compare [[Boiling]], [[Microwaving]]

**Steaming** cooks food in hot vapour above boiling water, so the food doesn't sit in water.

## What happens
- **Little leaching**: water-soluble vitamins and minerals mostly stay in the food.
- Steaming was the **only** method that did not significantly lower vitamin C in broccoli (Yuan et al. 2009).
- **Steaming spinach and broccoli caused no significant folate loss**, while boiling lost about half (McKillop et al. 2002).
- Minerals: Bognár (1998) grouped boiling and steaming together at 25–50% mineral loss; other data suggest steaming loses less than boiling. **Moderate.**
- Oxalate is removed less than with boiling (5–53% vs 30–87%; Chai & Liebman 2005).

## Typical retention
Vitamin C ~**70–95%**, folate ~**85–100%**, thiamin ~80–95%, minerals ~85–100%, carotenoids ~90–100% (absorption up).

## Filipino foods
Puto, kutsinta, suman (wrapped in leaves and steamed or boiled), siomai, pinasingawang isda, steamed lapu-lapu, steamed alimango/hipon, steamed okra and talong for ensalada, rice cakes. For kakanin, the nutrients are mostly starch, sugar and coconut; cooking changes them little.
