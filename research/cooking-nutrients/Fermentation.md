# Fermentation and pickling

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]]

**Fermentation** uses microbes (bacteria, yeasts, moulds) or the food's own enzymes to change food over days to months. Filipino examples: **patis** (fish sauce), **bagoong** isda and alamang, **burong isda** / **burong hipon** (fish or shrimp with rice, lactic fermentation), **suka** (vinegar), **tapuy**, **puto** made with fermented rice batter, **burong mangga**. **Atchara** is a pickle (vinegar-preserved papaya), usually *not* fermented today.

## What happens
- **Sodium is very high** in patis and bagoong. Salt is how they are preserved. Patis is commonly over 1,000 mg sodium per tablespoon (*secondary source*; check FNRI rows). This is a much bigger health issue than any vitamin change. UP Visayas researchers developed a lower-sodium mussel patis in 2025.
- **Protein is broken down** into peptides and free amino acids (the savoury umami taste). Protein value is mostly kept.
- **B vitamins** can increase from microbial growth (B2, B12 in some products), but reported B12 in fish sauce may be mostly **inactive analogues**; a Japanese study concluded fish sauce is not a reliable B12 source. **Weak evidence.**
- **Phytate falls** in fermented grains and legumes (puto from fermented rice, tapuy, tempeh-like products), raising iron and zinc absorption (Gibson, Perlas & Hotz 2006; Hotz & Gibson 2007).
- **Vitamin C** in pickles (atchara, burong mangga) falls over storage time; acid slows the loss. **Weak evidence, no Filipino data found.**

## For SarapSulit
- Fermented condiments are already priced and counted from their own FNRI rows (they are "as eaten" products). **No retention factor** should be applied to them.
- Sodium from patis, bagoong and toyo is already counted. A site warning for very-high-sodium dishes would be more useful than any retention change.
