# Vitamins D, E and K

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]]

Not tracked by SarapSulit today. All three are **fat-soluble**: they don't leach into water much, but they can **move into cooking fat** and can be **oxidized** by long, hot heating.

## Vitamin D
- Found in fatty fish (tamban/sardines, tuna, bangus belly), egg yolk, liver.
- Retention depends heavily on food and method. Jakobsen & Knuthsen (2014) measured eggs, bread and margarine: short boiling and frying kept most; **long oven baking kept the least** (roughly 40% in eggs baked for 40 min, *from memory of the paper; verify the exact figures*).
- Lešková 2006 notes that data on D are incomplete. **Weak / moderate evidence.**

## Vitamin E (tocopherols)
- Main sources: vegetable oils, nuts, seeds, green leaves.
- Destroyed by **oxidation**, especially in **frying oil that is reused** many times.
- Food fried in fresh oil **gains** vitamin E from the oil; Fillion & Henry 1998 conclude fried foods are generally a good source of it.
- Lee et al. 2018 measured **higher α-tocopherol after cooking** in some vegetables (better extraction).

## Vitamin K (phylloquinone, K1)
- Main sources: dark green leaves (malunggay, kangkong, pechay, saluyot, ampalaya leaves).
- **Fairly heat-stable.** Cooking often leaves it unchanged or measurably higher (better extraction), and boiling or stir-frying improved bioaccessibility in one study of leafy vegetables. In legumes, boiling lowered it (Korean 2023 study). **Moderate evidence.**
