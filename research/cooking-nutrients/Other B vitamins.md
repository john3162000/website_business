# Other B vitamins

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see also [[Thiamin (B1)]]

None of these is tracked by SarapSulit today. They are listed so a later version can add them.

| Vitamin | Heat | Water | Light | Typical cooking retention | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Riboflavin (B2)** | fairly stable | leaches | **very light-sensitive** | ~75–95% | Milk in clear bottles loses B2 in sunlight. |
| **Niacin (B3)** | **stable** | leaches | stable | ~75–100% | Losses are mostly into water or drippings (Lešková 2006). |
| **B6** | moderate | leaches | some | ~50–90% | Among the bigger hot-holding losses (Williams 1996). |
| **Folate** | **sensitive** (oxidation) | **leaches strongly** | some | boiled ~40–60%; steamed ~90–100% | McKillop et al. 2002: boiling left **49%** in spinach and **44%** in broccoli; **steaming caused no significant loss**. Lešková 2006: ~40% where leaching occurs. |
| **B12** | moderate | leaches into drippings | some | ~60–90% | Microwave heating destroyed **30–40%** of B12 in raw beef, pork and milk (Watanabe et al. 1998). Only in animal foods. |

**Well established:** folate behaves like vitamin C (leaching + oxidation); niacin is the most stable B vitamin.

**Filipino angle:** malunggay, kangkong, saluyot and monggo are folate sources. Steaming or short simmering in a broth that is eaten (tinola, ginisang monggo) keeps most of it. Liver (atay) and dinuguan are big B12 and folate sources; long simmering loses some.
