# Simmering and stewing

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · compare [[Boiling]], [[Pressure cooking]]

**Simmering** is cooking just below boiling (≈85–95 °C). **Stewing / braising** is long simmering in a small amount of liquid that is eaten with the food (adobo, kaldereta, menudo, afritada, mechado, paksiw, pochero, pinakbet).

## What happens
- **Liquid is eaten**, so leached minerals and water-soluble vitamins stay in the dish.
- **Time is long** (30 min to 3 h for tough meat), so heat-sensitive vitamins keep breaking down: vitamin C, folate, thiamin. Vitamins in meat (B vitamins) partly move into the sauce.
- **Acid** (vinegar in adobo and paksiw, tomato in kaldereta/afritada, tamarind in sinigang) **protects** vitamin C and thiamin.
- **Tomato sauce** gains available lycopene (Dewanto 2002: +171% trans-lycopene after 15 min at 88 °C) while losing ~30% vitamin C after 30 min.
- Bognár (1998): stewing kept B1, B2, B6 and C at **lower** levels than frying in most cases.

## Typical retention (stewed dish, sauce eaten)
- Vitamin C: ~**40–70%** (lower with longer time).
- Thiamin: ~**50–80%**; other B vitamins ~70–90%.
- Minerals: ~**95–100%**.
- Vitamin A/carotenoids: ~**75–95%**.
- Protein, carbs, fiber: ~100%; fat: depends on skimming.

**Moderate evidence:** few studies measure Filipino stews directly; values are inferred from boiling/stewing data (USDA 2007, Bognár 2002 tables, Lešková 2006).

## Vegetables added at the end
In pinakbet, sinigang and nilaga, many cooks add the greens last. Those vegetables get a **short simmer** (see [[Boiling]], blanching row) even though the meat stews for an hour. The engine should give them the short-cook factor. See [[Applying it to SarapSulit]].
