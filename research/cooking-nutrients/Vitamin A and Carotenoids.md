# Vitamin A and carotenoids

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see also [[Myths and facts]]

## Two kinds of "vitamin A"

- **Retinol (preformed vitamin A)** comes from animal foods: liver, egg yolk, milk, fish. It is fat-soluble and fairly heat-stable, but oxidizes with long heating and air. Lešková et al. 2006 list retinol among the more labile vitamins (≈33% retention reported in some boiled-vegetable data; meat and egg dishes usually keep much more).
- **Provitamin A carotenoids**, mainly **beta-carotene**, come from plants: kalabasa, carrots, kamote, malunggay, kangkong, saluyot, mango, papaya. The body turns them into vitamin A. Other carotenoids (lycopene in tomatoes, lutein in greens) are not vitamin A but are useful antioxidants.

FNRI reports one "vitamin A" number in retinol equivalents that combines both.

## The myth: "vitamin A disappears with heat"

**False for plant foods.** Two things happen at once:

1. **Content drops a little.** Heat and oxygen destroy some carotenoids and turn some into less active forms (cis-isomers). Typical retention in cooked vegetables: **~70–100%**. Some studies even measure **more than 100%**, because heat releases carotenoids that the lab could not extract from the raw tissue (Lee et al. 2018 found 40–125%; a Malaysian study of leafy greens including kangkong found stir-frying raised measured beta-carotene 2–3×).
2. **Availability goes up, often a lot.** Raw plant cell walls trap carotenoids. Cooking, mashing and chopping break them open. Fat dissolves them so they can be absorbed.

## Evidence that cooking and fat help

- **Rock et al. 1998:** women eating cooked, puréed carrots and spinach had plasma beta-carotene rises about **3×** those eating the same amount raw (small study, n = 8, P = 0.09, so *moderate* evidence).
- **Livny et al. 2003:** in ileostomy volunteers, **65%** of beta-carotene was absorbed from cooked puréed carrots vs **41%** from raw chopped carrots.
- **Dewanto et al. 2002:** heating tomatoes at 88 °C raised measurable trans-lycopene from 2.01 to 5.45 mg/g after 15 min (**+171%**), while vitamin C fell 29% after 30 min.
- **Brown et al. 2004:** salads eaten with full-fat dressing gave **2–4×** more carotenoid absorption than reduced-fat dressing; fat-free gave almost none.

**Well established:** carotenoids need fat to be absorbed, and gentle cooking raises how much is absorbed.

## Losses still happen

- Long, repeated boiling and stirring: dried drumstick (malunggay) leaves lost **~65%** beta-carotene in recipes with repeated boiling; steaming **~27%**, shallow frying **~31%** (Indian study of dehydrated drumstick leaves, *secondary summary; verify*).
- Deep-frying at high heat for a long time and reused oil increase losses.
- Light and air during sun-drying destroy carotenoids.

## Filipino angle

- **Ginataang kalabasa at sitaw**, **ginataang langka**, **laing** (taro leaves in coconut milk): coconut fat makes carotenoids much more absorbable. See [[Filipino dishes and methods]].
- **Ginisa** (sautéed in oil) vegetables: same benefit.
- Tinola with malunggay or dahon ng sili: little fat, but chicken skin fat helps.

## For SarapSulit

- **Retention factor for vitamin A content: ~0.8–1.0** for most methods. Losses are small compared to vitamin C.
- **Do not fold bioavailability into the retention factor.** Retention is about content; absorption is a separate, more uncertain number. A site note ("eaten with fat or gata, so more is absorbed") is honest; a bigger number is not. See [[Applying it to SarapSulit]].
