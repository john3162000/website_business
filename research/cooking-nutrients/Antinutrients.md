# Antinutrients

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see also [[Iron]], [[Calcium]], [[Protein]]

**Antinutrients** are natural compounds in plants that block the absorption of minerals or the digestion of protein. Cooking, soaking and fermenting reduce most of them. This is one reason cooking can **improve** nutrition even when it lowers vitamin content.

## Oxalate (oxalic acid)
- In: spinach, alugbati, kulitis (amaranth), saluyot, kangkong (some), taro leaves (gabi, laing), kamias, balimbing.
- Binds calcium and iron; high intake raises kidney-stone risk.
- **Boiling cut soluble oxalate by 30–87%; steaming by 5–53%; baking (potatoes) by none.** Almost all the lost oxalate was found in the cooking water (Chai & Liebman 2005).
- **Laing / gabi leaves:** also contain **calcium oxalate crystals** (raphides) that make the throat itchy; long simmering in gata, without stirring, is the traditional answer. *Traditional practice; little Filipino-specific data.*

## Phytate (phytic acid)
- In: rice bran, whole grains, monggo and other legumes, peanuts, seeds.
- Binds iron, zinc and calcium in the gut.
- **Soaking, germinating (togue/bean sprouts), fermenting and cooking** lower phytate and raise absorption of Fe, Zn and Ca (Gibson, Perlas & Hotz 2006; Hotz & Gibson 2007). Ordinary boiling alone removes only part; combining methods works best.
- Leah Perlas, a co-author, is from FNRI-DOST: this work is directly relevant to Filipino diets.

## Lectins
- In: raw beans, especially **red kidney beans** (phytohaemagglutinin).
- As few as 4–5 raw kidney beans can cause vomiting. **Boiling at 100 °C destroys the lectin** (10 min is enough in studies; FDA advises 30 min to be safe). **Heating only to ~80 °C** (slow cookers) can make the beans **more** toxic than raw (FDA Bad Bug Book).
- Filipino dishes with beans (pork and beans, *kadyos* in KBL, *habichuelas*) are normally boiled long enough.

## Trypsin inhibitors and tannins
- Trypsin inhibitors in raw soy and legumes block protein digestion; heat destroys them.
- Tannins/polyphenols (tea, coffee, some legumes) lower iron absorption; cooking changes them little.

## For SarapSulit
Antinutrients are not tracked. Their main effect is on **absorption**, which SarapSulit should not try to model. Good candidates for site tips: "boil leafy greens briefly", "soak monggo", "add calamansi or tomato for iron".
