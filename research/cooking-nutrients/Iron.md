# Iron

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see also [[Calcium]], [[Antinutrients]]

## Short answer

Iron is a mineral, so heat **cannot destroy it**. Cooking changes iron in three other ways:

1. **Leaching** into water that is thrown away (small to moderate loss; iron leaches less than potassium).
2. **Form change in meat:** heat turns some **heme iron** (the well-absorbed kind in meat and fish) into non-heme iron.
3. **Absorption changes:** cooking lowers phytate and oxalate, which raises absorption from plant foods; cooking in an **iron pot** adds iron.

## Numbers

- **Mineral losses overall:** in mass cooking, cooked foods kept on average **~60–75%** of their raw mineral content (Kimura & Itokawa 1990; the English and Japanese abstracts give 60–70% and ~75%). Losses were biggest when vegetables were **boiled and then squeezed** (as in Japanese ohitashi; compare Filipino *ensaladang talbos* where leaves are blanched and squeezed).
- **Boiling and steaming** reduced mineral content by **25–50%** in Bognár's comparison; **frying** kept minerals almost completely (Bognár 1998).
- **Boiling small potato cubes** lowered iron along with K, Mg, Zn and P (Bethke & Jansky 2008).
- **Heme iron in meat:** cooking reduced heme iron by **1–24%** for pan-cooked meats and **22–43%** in poultry, depending on the method (Lombardi-Boccia et al. 2002). Total iron stays; only the well-absorbed fraction shrinks.

## Absorption (bioavailability)

- **Iron pots:** Ethiopian children whose food was cooked in iron pots had haemoglobin rise **1.3 g/dL more** over a year than those using aluminium pots (Adish et al. 1999, Lancet). *Filipino cast-iron kawali may add iron too; not measured for Filipino dishes.*
- **Vitamin C** in the same meal raises non-heme iron absorption (calamansi, tomato in sinigang, kamias). See [[Vitamin C]].
- **Phytate removal** by soaking, fermenting and cooking raises iron absorption from grains and legumes (Gibson, Perlas & Hotz 2006, with Leah Perlas of FNRI; Hotz & Gibson 2007). See [[Antinutrients]].
- **Tea and coffee** with meals lower non-heme iron absorption (polyphenols).
