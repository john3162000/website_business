# Acid "cooking": kinilaw, kilawin, ceviche

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]]

**Kinilaw** is raw fish or seafood (tanigue, tuna, malasugi, talaba, tahong) "cooked" in vinegar (sukang tuba, sukang iloko) or calamansi, often with ginger, onion, chili and sometimes gata. **Kilawin** may also be lightly grilled meat dressed in vinegar.

## What happens
- **Acid denatures surface proteins** (the fish turns white and firm) without heat. Protein is not lost. Digestibility is probably similar to lightly cooked fish (*little direct data*). See [[Protein]].
- **No heat, no leaching water**, and acid **protects** vitamin C. Heat-sensitive vitamins (thiamin, B12, vitamin C from calamansi and onions) stay near **95–100%**.
- The marinating liquid is often **partly** eaten; some vinegar and calamansi is left in the bowl. SarapSulit's marinade rules may already apply.

## Food safety (important)
- Acid is **not** as reliable as heat. In a lab study of lime-juice ceviche, *Vibrio parahaemolyticus* fell below detection (~5-log), but *Salmonella* fell only ~1–2 log (Mathur & Schaffner 2013).
- Acid does **not** reliably kill parasites (Anisakis and others). Freezing the fish first is the standard advice (NCCEH ceviche report).
- Pregnant people, young children, the elderly and immune-compromised people should avoid it.

## For SarapSulit
Treat kinilaw as **raw** (retention ≈ 1.0 for all nutrients). A food-safety note in the recipe pop-up would be useful.
