# Carbohydrates, starch and fiber

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]]

## Total carbohydrate
Cooking hardly changes the **grams** of carbohydrate (retention ≈ 1.0). A little sugar leaches into broth, and some sugar is used up in browning (Maillard, caramelizing). What changes is **how fast** it is digested.

## Starch gelatinization
Raw starch (in raw rice, kamote, saba, cassava, flour) is packed in hard granules that enzymes digest slowly. Heating in water makes the granules swell and burst (**gelatinization**), so the starch becomes **fast to digest** and raises blood sugar faster. This is why cooked rice and cassava are digestible at all.

## Resistant starch (RS)
- **Resistant starch** is starch that escapes digestion in the small intestine and acts like fiber (Englyst et al. 1992 classification).
- When cooked starch **cools**, part of it re-crystallizes (**retrogradation**) and becomes resistant starch.
- **Rice:** fresh cooked white rice had **0.64 g RS/100 g**; rice cooled 10 h at room temperature had 1.30 g; rice **cooled 24 h at 4 °C then reheated** had **1.65 g**, and gave a lower blood-sugar response in 15 healthy adults (Sonia, Witjaksono & Ridwan 2015, Indonesia).
- **Practical meaning:** bahaw (left-over rice) and **sinangag** (fried day-old rice) have a bit more RS than fresh rice. The effect is real but small (about 1 g per 100 g).
- **Frying** potatoes raises measured fiber because resistant starch forms (Fillion & Henry 1998).
- **Food safety:** cooked rice left warm can grow *Bacillus cereus*. Cool it quickly and refrigerate. See [[Reheating and Leftovers]].

## Fiber
- Total dietary fiber changes little with cooking (retention ≈ 0.9–1.1). Cooking softens fiber and changes the soluble/insoluble split. Long boiling can move some soluble fiber (pectin) into the water.
- Measured fiber can rise after cooking because of resistant starch and better extraction.
