# Pressure cooking

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · compare [[Simmering and Stewing]], [[Boiling]]

A **pressure cooker** raises the boiling point to about 115–120 °C, so food cooks in a fraction of the time (bulalo or beef kaldereta in 30–45 min instead of 2–3 h).

## What happens
- **Higher temperature, much shorter time.** For heat-sensitive vitamins, the shorter time usually wins: retention is similar to or better than long simmering. Vitamin destruction depends on both time and temperature (Lešková 2006 kinetic models).
- **Less water and closed lid**: less leaching and less oxidation, but the liquid is usually eaten anyway in Filipino stews.
- **Legumes**: destroys lectins and trypsin inhibitors reliably and helps lower phytate (with prior soaking). See [[Antinutrients]].
- Softens fish bones (canned-sardine style), adding edible calcium. See [[Calcium]].

## Evidence quality
**Weak to moderate.** Many popular claims ("90% vitamin retention") could not be traced to a primary study during this research. Treat pressure-cooked stews like [[Simmering and Stewing]], with slightly higher vitamin retention.
