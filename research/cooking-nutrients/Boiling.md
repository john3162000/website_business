# Boiling

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · compare [[Steaming]], [[Simmering and Stewing]]

**Boiling** means cooking food in water at about 100 °C. **Blanching** is a very short boil (30 s to 3 min), often followed by cold water.

## The key question: is the water eaten?

| | Water **discarded** (drained) | Water **eaten** (soup, sabaw) |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin C | ~0–60% kept, often ~50% | ~60–80% kept |
| Folate | ~40–60% (McKillop 2002: 44–49%) | ~70–85% |
| Thiamin | ~50–80% | ~75–90% |
| Potassium | ~25–85% (cut size!) | ~95–100% |
| Fe, Ca, Mg | ~70–95% | ~95–100% |
| Carotenoids, vitamin K | ~80–100% (content); absorption up | same |
| Protein, carbs, fiber | ~95–100% | ~100% |
| Soluble oxalate (an antinutrient) | **30–87% removed** (good) | stays in the soup |

Sources: vitamin C (Lee et al. 2018; Yuan et al. 2009; USDA 2007 values via secondary source), folate (McKillop et al. 2002), potassium (Bethke & Jansky 2008: −50% diced, −75% shredded), minerals (Kimura & Itokawa 1990; Bognár 1998: boiling and steaming −25–50% minerals), oxalate (Chai & Liebman 2005). "Water eaten" values are reasoned from these: leaching is recovered, heat destruction is not. **Moderate evidence.**

## What drives the spread
- **Time** (minutes vs. an hour) and **temperature**.
- **Water-to-food ratio**: more water, more leaching.
- **Cut size**: small pieces and shredded food lose far more.
- Starting in **cold vs. boiling** water: dropping vegetables into already-boiling water shortens time and inactivates enzymes that destroy vitamin C.
- **Lid on** reduces time and oxygen.
- **Squeezing** boiled greens (ensalada) removes even more minerals (Kimura & Itokawa 1990).

## Filipino dishes
Nilaga, bulalo, tinola, sinigang, pesang isda, sopas, molo, binacol, utan/laswa, dinengdeng (broth eaten), and blanched ensalada greens or boiled saba/kamote/mais (water discarded). See [[Filipino dishes and methods]].
