# Filipino dishes and cooking methods

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see [[Retention table (summary)]], [[Applying it to SarapSulit]]

This table maps common Filipino dish styles to the cooking methods in these notes, with what to expect. Retention ranges are for vitamin C (the most sensitive nutrient SarapSulit tracks) unless stated. **Evidence level for Filipino dishes specifically: weak to moderate.** No FNRI-DOST study measuring nutrient retention in cooked Filipino dishes was found in this research (see "FNRI" below). Ranges are mapped from general data.

| **Ginisa** | ginisang monggo, ampalaya, upo, pechay | [[Sauteing and Stir-frying]] then short simmer | yes (if soupy) | 50–85% | ~95–100% | Oil helps carotenoid absorption. Monggo: soak first to lower phytate ([[Antinutrients]]). |

## Leafy greens added at the end

**Malunggay, kangkong, pechay, saluyot, talbos ng kamote, alugbati, dahon ng sili, ampalaya leaves.** These are the biggest vitamin C, folate and carotenoid sources in Filipino dishes.

- Added in the last 1–3 minutes, or after the heat is off: vitamin C ~**60–90%**, folate ~**70–90%**, carotenoids **~90–100%** (and more absorbed).
- Boiled 10+ minutes and drained: vitamin C ~**20–50%**, folate ~**40–50%**.
- Moringa (malunggay): cooking significantly lowered vitamin C and beta-carotene in Tanzanian work, though "considerable amounts" remained (Gidamis et al. 2003); blanching Moringa lost 21–50% of vitamin C in a Bangladeshi study (*secondary summary*).
- Water spinach (kangkong): boiling and frying lowered vitamin C significantly (Bangladeshi study); stir-frying raised measured beta-carotene 2–3× (Malaysian study, *extraction effect*).

## What FNRI-DOST has published

- **Philippine Food Composition Tables (PhilFCT)**, online at https://i.fnri.dost.gov.ph/login/fct (about 1,500–1,600 foods, free). Mostly **raw** foods, but it has some **cooked** rows: boiled rice (A020), boiled egg, fried pork belly (F149), roasted pork belly (F269), canned adobo (R050), boiled miki noodles. SarapSulit already uses several of these. **Those rows already include cooking losses; never apply a retention factor on top.**
- FNRI's 2019 FCT update added sodium, cholesterol, sugars, dietary fiber and fatty acids.
- FNRI's popular materials (Nutritional Guidelines for Filipinos, Menu Guide Calendar, "Bahay Kubo" articles) advise not overcooking vegetables and keeping the cooking water, but **no FNRI table of retention factors or cooked-dish measurements was found**.
- FNRI researcher **Leah Perlas** co-authored the review on household methods (soaking, fermentation, germination) to improve mineral absorption from plant foods (Gibson, Perlas & Hotz 2006).
- **Gap:** a short FNRI or university study measuring vitamin C and beta-carotene in sinigang, tinola, ginisang monggo and laing, raw vs cooked, would be very valuable. This could be a student project.
