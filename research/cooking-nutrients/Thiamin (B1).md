# Thiamin (vitamin B1)

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see also [[Other B vitamins]]

SarapSulit does not track thiamin today, but it matters for a rice-based diet.

## What happens

- Water-soluble, so it **leaches** into cooking water.
- **Heat-sensitive**, and much more so in **alkaline** conditions (baking soda added to keep vegetables green, or to soften beans, destroys it). It is fairly stable in acid.
- Chlorine in tap water speeds up the loss (Kimura et al. 1990).

## Numbers

- **Cooked Japanese daily meals held only about 50–60% of the thiamin calculated from raw-food tables** (Kimura, Itokawa & Fujiwara 1990). Losses were largest in **rice** and **green vegetables**. By method: boiling > baking > parching > frying.
- Typical retention across methods: **20–80%** (Lešková et al. 2006).
- Frying usually keeps more B1 than boiling, steaming or stewing (Bognár 1998).

**Well established:** thiamin intake calculated from raw-food tables is overstated.

## Rice

Most thiamin in milled rice sits near the surface. **Washing rice several times** before cooking and **cooking in excess water then draining** both remove thiamin. The usual Filipino method (absorption method, rice cooker, no draining) keeps more. FNRI publishes a cooked-rice row (A020), which SarapSulit already uses for `rice_white_cooked`, so cooked-rice losses are already inside that row. Do not apply a retention factor on top of it.
