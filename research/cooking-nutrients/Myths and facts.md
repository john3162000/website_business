# Myths and facts

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]]

| Belief | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Vitamins A and C disappear with heat." | **Half true.** | **Vitamin C** is very heat- and water-sensitive: boiling and draining can lose half or more (Lee et al. 2018; [[Vitamin C]]). **Carotenoids (plant vitamin A)** lose only a little content and become **more** absorbable when cooked with fat (Rock et al. 1998; Dewanto et al. 2002; Brown et al. 2004; [[Vitamin A and Carotenoids]]). |
| "Raw vegetables are always healthier." | **False.** | Raw is best for vitamin C and folate; cooked is better for carotenoids, lycopene, protein digestibility, and lower oxalate and lectins ([[Antinutrients]]). Eat both. |
| "Cooking destroys minerals." | **False.** | Minerals can't be destroyed. They leach into water. Eat the sabaw and you keep them (Kimura & Itokawa 1990; [[Iron]]). |
| "Microwaving kills nutrients." | **False.** | For vegetables, microwaving is one of the best methods for vitamin C (Lee et al. 2018). B12 in meat does fall 30–40% (Watanabe et al. 1998). [[Microwaving]] |
| "Fried food has no nutrients." | **False.** | Frying keeps protein and minerals and often more vitamins than boiling (Fillion & Henry 1998; Bognár 1998). Its problem is extra fat and calories. [[Deep frying]] |
| "Cooking protein destroys it." | **False.** | Protein is kept and usually digested better (cooked egg 91% vs raw 51%; Evenepoel et al. 1998). [[Protein]] |
| "Steaming is always best." | **Mostly true for water-soluble vitamins.** | Steaming kept vitamin C and folate best (Yuan 2009; McKillop 2002), but removes less oxalate than boiling. [[Steaming]] |
| "Kinilaw is cooked, so it's safe." | **False.** | Acid does not reliably kill Salmonella or parasites. [[Acid cooking (kinilaw)]] |
| "Day-old rice has fewer calories." | **Small truth.** | It gains ~1 g resistant starch per 100 g (Sonia et al. 2015). The calorie change is tiny. [[Carbohydrates, Starch and Fiber]] |
