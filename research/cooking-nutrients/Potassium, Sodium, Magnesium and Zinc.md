# Potassium, sodium, magnesium and zinc

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see also [[Iron]], [[Calcium]]

## Potassium (K)
- The **most easily leached** mineral.
- **Boiling diced potato cut potassium by 50%; boiling shredded potato by 75%**. Soaking alone did little (Bethke & Jansky 2008). Doctors use this "double-boiling" trick for kidney patients.
- If the broth is eaten (nilaga, sinigang), nearly all the potassium is still eaten.
- Retention: **~0.5–0.85** when boiled and drained (higher for whole pieces); **~1.0** when the liquid is eaten, or for frying, baking, roasting.

## Sodium (Na)
- Most sodium in Filipino dishes is **added**: salt, patis, toyo, bagoong, alamang, bouillon cubes. Cooking does not destroy it.
- **Salted water that is drained** leaves only part of its salt in the food. Pasta or vegetables boiled in salted water absorb some salt; most goes down the drain.
- **Marinades that are discarded** also take most of their salt away. SarapSulit already handles this with `marinadeFate` (marinade discarded, drained, basted).
- **Salting fish then frying (daing, tuyo)**: the salt is in the product; dried fish rows in FNRI already include it.
- **Soaking dried or salted fish** (tuyo, bacalao, salted eggs not soaked) lowers sodium. *Not measured for Filipino products in the sources found.*
- Fermented products are very salty. See [[Fermentation]].

## Magnesium (Mg)
- Leaches like potassium but less. Boiling potatoes reduced Mg (Bethke & Jansky 2008). Retention ~0.7–0.9 when drained.

## Zinc (Zn)
- Leaches little. The bigger issue is **absorption**: phytate in grains and legumes binds zinc. Soaking, fermenting, germinating and cooking reduce phytate and raise zinc absorption (Gibson, Perlas & Hotz 2006). See [[Antinutrients]].
- Meat and shellfish (talaba/oysters, tahong) are the best zinc sources, little affected by cooking.

## For SarapSulit
SarapSulit tracks **sodium** only (of these four). Sodium needs **no heat retention factor**. The useful rules are about **water or marinade thrown away**, which already exist for marinades. A "salted boiling water drained" rule could be added later; it matters for pasta (sopas, spaghetti) and boiled saba/kamote.
