# Vitamin C

Back to [[00 Index - Cooking and Nutrients]] · see also [[Retention table (summary)]]

## Short answer

Vitamin C is the **most fragile** nutrient SarapSulit tracks. It does not "disappear" with any heat, but it is lost in two ways at once:

1. **Leaching** into cooking water. It dissolves easily in water.
2. **Destruction** by heat and oxygen (oxidation). Losses grow with time, temperature, cut surface, and contact with air. Copper and iron ions and alkaline water (baking soda) speed it up. Acid (sinigang souring, vinegar, calamansi) slows it down.

**Well established:** vitamin C, folate and thiamin are the most cooking-sensitive vitamins (Lešková et al. 2006).

## How much is kept, by method

| Method | Typical retention | Notes and source |
|---|---|---|
| [[Boiling]], water drained | ~0–60%, often ~45–55% | Leafy greens boiled and drained ≈ 55% (USDA 2007, *secondary-source figure, verify*); Lee et al. 2018 found 0–91% across methods, with boiling the lowest. Long boiling of greens can leave almost none. |
| Boiling, broth eaten | ~60–80% | Leached vitamin C stays in the soup, but heat keeps destroying some. ~70% for "kept cooking water" (USDA, secondary figure). |
| [[Steaming]] | ~70–95% | The only method that did not significantly reduce broccoli vitamin C in Yuan et al. 2009. |
| [[Microwaving]] (little water) | ~70–95% | Generally the best or second-best in Lee et al. 2018. |
| [[Sauteing and Stir-frying]] | ~50–85% | Yuan 2009: stir-frying and stir-fry-then-boil gave significant losses in broccoli. Short cooking of cut leaves keeps more. |
| [[Deep frying]] | often better than boiling | Bognár 1998 and Fillion & Henry 1998: frying usually kept more vitamin C than boiling or stewing; French fries keep vitamin C near raw-potato levels. |
| Long [[Simmering and Stewing]] (tomato sauce, 30 min at 88 °C) | ~70% | Dewanto et al. 2002: tomato vitamin C fell 29% after 30 min at 88 °C. |
| Hot-holding (warm in a pot for hours) | >10% extra loss after 2 h | Williams 1996 review. |
| Chilled 24 h then reheated | >30% extra loss | Williams 1996. See [[Reheating and Leftovers]]. |
| [[Acid cooking (kinilaw)]] | ~90–100% (no heat) | Acid protects vitamin C. *Logical inference, little direct data.* |

**What drives the spread:** cooking time (the biggest factor), amount of water (more water = more leaching), cut size (small pieces lose more), whether the lid is on, pH, and the starting freshness of the food.

## Practical facts for Filipino cooking

- Add **malunggay, kangkong, pechay, saluyot, talbos ng kamote** at the end and cook for 1–3 minutes. See [[Filipino dishes and methods]].
- In **sinigang, tinola, nilaga**, the broth is eaten, so leaching is mostly not a loss.
- Raw fruit (mango, calamansi, guava, papaya) and *ensalada* keep all their vitamin C.
- Vitamin C also **helps iron absorption** from plant foods. See [[Iron]].

## For SarapSulit

Today the engine counts raw vitamin C. For boiled-and-drained or long-stewed vegetable dishes it probably **overstates vitamin C by about 1.5–3×**. This is the single largest cooking error in the current numbers. See [[Applying it to SarapSulit]].
